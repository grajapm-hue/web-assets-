MathMatrix Pro++ · design system v2

Less colour, more meaning — every screen in one system

The last pass gave nine puzzles nine saturated gradients. That reads as a toy, and it made every tile shout at the same volume. Here colour carries information instead of decoration: hue tells you the mode, depth tells you the difficulty, and one warm gold carries every action and reward. Playful shapes, generous radii and squishy feedback do the work of being kid-friendly.

1 · The palette, cut from nine hues to four

Before — 9 competing gradients

Nine full-bleed fills. Nothing is emphasised because everything is. No colour means anything — they are just labels.

After — 4 mode hues + 1 accent

Blue = add · violet = multiply · rose = birthday · cyan = binary. Gold is action and reward, green is “solved”. One screen shows one mode, so you rarely see more than two hues at once.

The neutrals are indigo-biased, not grey — they belong to the same family as the accent, which is what stops a dark UI looking like a dead browser default.

Type & rhythm

The app is a single offline file with no CDN, so a webfont is not available — hierarchy has to come from weight and size, not from a display face. Scale: 22 / 16 / 14 / 12.5 / 10.5 px. Weights: 900 titles, 800 controls, 600 body. All digits use tabular-nums so a timer or a grid never jitters. Spacing runs on a 4px rhythm; radii are 10 (cells) / 15 (cards) / 20 (sheets).

2 · Every screen

Nine screens at true phone width. Same tokens throughout — the only thing that changes between games is the mode hue.

Neutral on purpose. Ten digits in gold would fight the cursor ring and the child would not know where to look. The keys stay quiet so the one gold thing on screen is the box waiting for a number. Utility keys sit a step back again — dimmer fill, lighter weight — because ⌫ and the arrows are help, not the main job.

Sizing. Keys are 1.32:1 and never below 44px tall, with 5px between them, so a small finger cannot hit two at once. The pressed state sinks the key inward rather than moving it, so nothing shifts under the thumb. ± only appears where negatives are possible, and it borrows the mode hue rather than introducing a new one.

This was missing and it mattered. The corners of the badge ring were blank, so two of the eight lines had no tracker at all — a child could fill every row and column and still be wrong with nothing on screen to say why.

All four corners, both ends. Every row and column total already shows at both ends of the ring, so a diagonal marked only at the top would be the odd one out — and on a 10×10 board that total is nowhere near the hand working the bottom. Each corner therefore carries its diagonal's total, with the arrow pointing into the grid along that line. The app already marks four corners; what changes is that they are tagged by direction rather than by a magenta that means nothing. The green diagonal guide also only appears on 5×5 today — it belongs on every board.

9How to playrules, before the first move
←How to play 3×3

The goal

Fill every box with 1 to 9, using each number once, so that every row, every column and both diagonals add up to the same total.

The count-down method
1
Put 1 in the top middle

Always start there. The rest follows a single rule.

2
Count three down, place to the right

Wrap around to the top when you fall off the bottom.

3
Blocked? Drop straight below

If that box is taken, put the number under the last one instead.

4
Repeat until 9

The square finishes itself. Every line will match.

12Learnthe maths behind it
←💡 Math Logic

The secret structure

The middle box is always target ÷ 3. Opposite boxes always add to twice the middle — for 5 that's 10: 1+9, 2+8, 3+7, 4+6.

Eight twins

Rotate or mirror a solved square and all eight versions stay magic. The numbers move; the relationships never do.

Why binary works

Every digit 0–9 is built from 8, 4, 2 and 1. That is the whole trick — the cards are just those four weights, twice.

3 · Rules this system runs on

1
Gold is spent once per screen

One primary action, or one reward. If two things are gold, neither is primary.

2
Hue = mode, depth = difficulty

A new colour is only justified by a new kind of maths, never by a new size.

3
Never signal with colour alone

Solved cells change fill and gain a tint; selected cards get an outline as well as a wash. A colour-blind child loses nothing.

4
Every tap answers within 100ms

Squish to 93% on press, spring back. Motion is 150–300ms, and all of it stops under prefers-reduced-motion.

5
Touch targets ≥ 44px, always

Including the small cells on 10×10, which extend their hit area beyond their visible box.

Kid-friendliness is carried by form and feedback — big radii, squishy presses, a bouncing monkey, confetti on a win — rather than by saturation. That is the difference between playful and noisy, and it is also what lets an adult use it without embarrassment.
