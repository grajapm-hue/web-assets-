PALLANGUZHI — RULES AS THEY STAND (17 Aug 2026)

==========================================================================
PART 1 — ALREADY SETTLED, BY RAJA PLAYING IT
==========================================================================
These were open questions. He answered them by playing the beta and telling me
what was wrong, which is a better way to settle a rule than a questionnaire.

1. PASU — a cup of exactly FOUR may be taken only by the player whose ROW it
   sits in. They must tap it themselves. If a fifth seed falls in, the chance is
   gone for good. Claimable AT ANY TIME, including while the opponent is still
   sowing — this is the assumption I made and it has stood; say if it is wrong.
   [built]

2. SOWING — lift a cup, drop one seed at a time round the board. When the hand
   empties, seeds in the next cup are lifted and the move carries on. An empty
   next cup ends the move: the cup BEYOND it is won. Two empty in a row wins
   nothing. [built]

3. WHO OPENS — a choice, not a rule. Either player may be set to open, because
   the opener picks the cup whose reach ends where they want it. [built]

4. LAYING OUT — each player taps their OWN store of 35 and lays out their OWN
   seven cups, five at a time. No automatic fill, no fixed order. [built]

5. DIRECTION — one continuous ring, leaving cup 7 of one row for cup 1 of the
   other. [built]

6. SPEED — one steady beat, every drop, slow enough to spot a cup of four and
   claim it. [built]

PART 2 — பிள்ளை பாண்டி / PILLAI PAANDI — WHAT I UNDERSTOOD
==========================================================================
Raja's words, 17 Aug: "if any one pocket is empty, any seeds in opposite pocket
same will own by opposite of empty area and game restart by fill box each 5 in
by with in who holds reserve. If who shortage one, leave there pocket should
empty and no one fill the seeds those pockets. If less than 5 in hand and same
kept it in empty pocket. And who owns the 4 or 3 or 2 or 1, those only fill
every time what they kept in initial seeds, not need fill to opponent along
empty pocket any. Vice versa until end of game, by any one may loose there
entire pocket. This is called pillai paandi."

WHAT I THINK THIS SAYS:

  A. A round ends, and then each player REFILLS THEIR OWN ROW from the seeds
     they won — five to a cup, exactly as the opening lay-out already works.

  B. A player who runs SHORT cannot fill a cup with five. That cup is left
     EMPTY and it is DEAD: nobody ever fills it again. This is the pillai
     paandi — a hole lost out of your own row.

  C. Any remainder of fewer than five goes into that empty cup.

  D. A cup left holding 4, 3, 2 or 1 keeps THAT number as its filling from then
     on, not five.

  E. You only ever fill your OWN cups. Never the opponent's, and never a dead
     one.

  F. The game ends when a player has lost their whole row.

  G. A PART-FILLED CUP BELONGS TO WHOEVER FILLED IT. Raja, 17 Aug: "the semi
     empty filled pocket is end of game own to who filled in play." So the seeds
     sitting in a cup that was laid out with 4, 3, 2 or 1 are not neutral and do
     not go to whoever happens to be nearest — at the end of the game they count
     for the player who put them there. Which also means a cup keeps its owner
     even when it is holding less than a full five.
     [This answers a question I had not thought to ask: what becomes of seeds
      still on the board when the game ends. They are not left out of the count.]

PART 2b — ALL FOUR ANSWERED, 17 Aug. ALL OF IT IS NOW BUILT.
==========================================================================
Q1 "seeds in the opposite pocket" is NOT a capture during play. When one side
   runs dry the OTHER side keeps its own remaining seeds, into its own reserve.
   Nothing changes hands, and the capture rule already built is untouched.

Q2 A ROUND ENDS the moment the player to move has no playable seeds in their
   own row.

Q3 THE REFILL IS WHAT THE RESERVE AFFORDS, worked out fresh every round: fives
   while fives can be afforded, then one cup holding the remainder, then
   empties. Anything above 35 stays in the reserve box.

Q4 THERE IS NO SUCH THING AS A DEAD CUP — my question was built on a wrong
   assumption, and asking what I meant by it is how that came out. An empty cup
   is empty for THAT ROUND ONLY. Win seeds back and you lay it out again.

AND THE RULE THAT MAKES THE GAME WHAT IT IS — THE SAVINGS POCKET:
   The under-five remainder cup is a பிள்ளை / pillai pocket, of exactly that
   size for the round.
     · Nobody may lift from it. Nobody may capture it.
     · A sower passing it must pay it ITS OWN SIZE, not one seed.
     · A sower who does not hold that much STOPS THERE.
     · At the end of the round its whole contents go back to the reserve of the
       player whose row it sits in.
   Raja: "it's like savings in bank... this rule is for forced savings, to hold
   low seeds in end of sequence; this surplus will help them to fill his all
   pockets with 5 seeds." A player who came up short one round is being made to
   put something aside, and it is what brings them back.

PART 3 — THE QUESTIONS AS THEY WERE ASKED (all now answered above)
==========================================================================
Each of these gives a DIFFERENT GAME depending on the answer, so I would rather
ask than invent your family's rules.

Q1. "IF ANY ONE POCKET IS EMPTY, ANY SEEDS IN OPPOSITE POCKET WILL BE OWNED BY
    THE OPPOSITE OF THE EMPTY AREA."
    I cannot tell which of these you mean:
      (a) DURING PLAY: when my last seed lands in an empty cup of mine, I win
          the seeds in the cup FACING it across the board. (A capture rule —
          and different from the one already built, which takes the cup beyond
          an empty one.)
      (b) AT THE END OF A ROUND: any cup left empty means the seeds facing it
          are collected before the refill.
      (c) Something else.
    → Which one, and does it REPLACE the capture rule already built or sit
      alongside it?

Q2. WHEN EXACTLY DOES A ROUND END?
      (a) When the player to move has no seeds anywhere in their own row.
      (b) When the whole board is empty of seeds.
      (c) Something else.

Q3. THE REMAINDER OF FEWER THAN FIVE — NARROWED, NOT CLOSED.
    Say a player has 23 seeds: four cups of five, and 3 left over.
    Point G above settles who those 3 seeds BELONG to at the end — the player
    who laid them out. What it does not settle is what happens to that cup in
    the rounds BETWEEN:
      (a) It is a 3-cup from now on, filled with 3 every round after.
      (b) It holds 3 this round only, and goes back to five as soon as that
          player can afford five again.
    → (a) makes a bad round permanent and the game shorter and sharper.
      (b) lets a player recover, and the game runs longer.
      Which is it in your family?

Q4. CAN A DEAD CUP EVER COME BACK?
      (a) No — once lost, lost for the whole game.
      (b) Yes, if that player later has enough seeds to fill it again.
    And: do seeds still get SOWN THROUGH a dead cup as they pass, or is it
    skipped entirely?
