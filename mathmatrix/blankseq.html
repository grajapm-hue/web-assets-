Fill In The Blank — any nine in a row

The shipped puzzle is fixed to 1–9. This trial asks whether it works with nine consecutive numbers starting anywhere — 2–10, 5–13, 10–18 — so multi-digit numbers appear on their own and the six answers are worked out behind the play.

Does it hold up? The magic square accepts any run because its property survives shifting. This one mixes + − × ÷, which do not shift together, so every start has to be searched for on its own. The real unknown is ÷: every step must stay whole and positive, and 12 ÷ 7 does not land. Measure it rather than guess.
