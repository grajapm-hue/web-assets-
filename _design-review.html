What makes each tile different now

1
A dot grid at its real size

Look at the small square on the left of each card. 3×3 is nine fat dots; 10×10 is a fine mesh. That alone separates the six tiles that used to look identical — no reading required.

2
The numbers it uses

1–9, 1–16, 1–25, 1–36, 1–64, 1–100. Different on every card, and it tells a child how big the job is before they start.

3
Its method

count-down · fill & swap · four blocks · fill & flip. This also quietly teaches that 3×3 and 5×5 share a method, and 6×6 and 10×10 share another.

One thing I left off on purpose

No target numbers on the cards

Printing “every line = 15” would have been the easiest way to make the tiles look different — and it would have wrecked the discovery the game is built on. The target stays ? until two lines agree and the player works it out. There is now a test that fails if 15, 34, 65, 111, 260 or 505 ever appears on the chooser.

Your call on one detail

“Choose Difficulty” used to be the red vertical label down the side. With four separate mode groups it had nothing to stand beside, so I kept the words and moved them to the top as a heading. If you want the vertical label back, it can return on the Add Magic group only — that six-rung ladder is the only place “difficulty” really applies.
